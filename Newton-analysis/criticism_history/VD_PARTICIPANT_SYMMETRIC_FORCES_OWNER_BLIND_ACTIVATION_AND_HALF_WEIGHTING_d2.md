## Owner-Blind Activation, Same-Kind Reciprocity, and Half-Weighting

**Status:** discussion note / candidate theorem, d2  
**Date:** 13 August 2026  
**Project:** VD Newton

## 0. Purpose

This note records one connected line of reasoning about gravity, Coulomb interaction, and participant-symmetric force kinds in the Valid Dictionary.

The argument has three stages.

1. A participant-symmetric VD implementation represents one gravitational or Coulomb pair interaction from both participant-owned sides. If each representation returns the full ordinary pair-force, the force is counted twice. Each representation must therefore carry one half of the ordinary pair-force.

2. The double representation is not an arbitrary implementation choice. It follows naturally when a single acting-object kind has an **owner-blind activation condition** and obeys **same-kind reciprocity**: if an `A`-owned instance acts on `B`, then `B` can own the same acting-object kind and act reciprocally on `A`.

3. Under those rules, cross-type participation forces same-type closure. If `A` and `B` can interact through one participant-symmetric acting-object kind, then distinct `A`-type participants can interact with one another, and distinct `B`-type participants can interact with one another.

The core simplification over the previous draft is this:

> The source–target product structure is no longer assumed separately. It is generated by the type of the activation condition itself.

The note also records two boundaries:

- the theorem first proves that a same-type force occurrence is **admitted**, not that its value must be nonzero in every state;
- owner-blind activation is proposed here for participant-symmetric fundamental acting-objects, not yet for springs, contacts, and every other acting-object in the VD.

# Part I — The Acting-Object Interface

## 1. Owner, non-owner, condition, and value

Take one acting-object kind `G`.

An instance of `G` is owned by one participant. Call that participant the **owner**. The participant on which the owner's `G` may act is the **non-owner** for that occurrence.

The architecture separates three questions:

```text
1. Can x own an instance of G?
2. Does y satisfy G's activation condition?
3. If so, what force value does x's G return on y?
```

Write these schematically as:

$$
\operatorname{Own}_G(x),
$$

$$
\operatorname{Condition}_G(y),
$$

and

$$
V_G(x,y).
$$

The proposed typing rule is:

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Condition}_G:
\mathrm{NonOwner}\rightarrow\{\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false}\}
}
$$

while

$$
\boxed{
V_G:
\mathrm{Owner}\times\mathrm{NonOwner}\rightarrow\mathbb V_F.
}
$$

Thus:

> **The activation condition may query only the prospective non-owner. The force-value rule may query both the owner and the non-owner.**

The owner is not physically irrelevant. Its mass, charge, current, position, and other state may all enter the force calculation. What is forbidden is letting the identity or type of the owner change which activation condition is being run.

For two distinct participants `x` and `y`, a `G` occurrence from `x` to `y` is admitted when

```text
x can own G
AND
y passes Condition_G.
```

Only after admission does the value procedure calculate the vector from the joint state. The theorem below is stated at the level of participant **types or condition-relevant qualification classes**. Its diagonal therefore means interaction between distinct instances of one type, not literal self-force.

## 2. Why the restriction matters

Without this restriction, a purportedly single acting-object could secretly contain several different target rules:

```text
if owner is Red:
    accept Blue targets

if owner is Blue:
    accept Red targets
```

Before evaluating the condition, the interpreter would have to inspect the owner and select a different activation procedure. The single name would conceal two callable kinds.

Under the owner-blind rule, that structure must be exposed as two acting-object kinds:

```text
red-to-blue acting-object
    owner qualification: Red
    activation condition(non-owner): Blue

blue-to-red acting-object
    owner qualification: Blue
    activation condition(non-owner): Red
```

The reciprocal forces may still be equal and opposite. But they are not generated by participant exchange within one acting-object kind.

This gives a VD-native criterion for the granularity of an acting-object kind:

> If changing the owner requires selecting a different activation condition, the supposed single acting-object kind is reducible and should be split.

Different owner data may change the **output** of one force procedure. It may not select a different activation condition while remaining one irreducible kind.

## 3. Atomicity for the present theorem

The theorem below applies to an acting-object kind at the smallest level at which the following are fixed:

```text
one owner qualification
one owner-blind activation condition
one force-value procedure
```

A broad label may collect several such kinds as a family. Reciprocity at the family label is not enough. The reciprocal occurrence must preserve the same atomic acting-object kind.

This is what prevents the two rules

```text
red-to-blue
blue-to-red
```

from being hidden under the super-label `purple-force` and then treated as one participant-symmetric mechanism.

# Part II — Why Gravity and Coulomb Force Are Halved in the VD

## 4. The ordinary one-sided presentation

A standard calculation usually chooses one participant as source and the other as target.

For Newtonian gravity, the ordinary physical pair-force on `B` due to `A` is

$$
\mathbf F^{\mathrm{grav}}_{A\to B}
=
Gm_A m_B
\frac{\mathbf r_A-\mathbf r_B}
{\lVert\mathbf r_A-\mathbf r_B\rVert^3}.
$$

For point-charge electrostatics, the Coulomb pair-force is

$$
\mathbf F^{\mathrm C}_{A\to B}
=
\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}
q_Aq_B
\frac{\mathbf r_B-\mathbf r_A}
{\lVert\mathbf r_B-\mathbf r_A\rVert^3},
$$

with the charge signs determining attraction or repulsion.

The ordinary presentation evaluates one direction and obtains the reverse force by the equal-and-opposite relation.

The participant-symmetric VD implementation instead gives each participant an instance of the same force-kind:

```text
A owns G_A.
B owns G_B.
```

For gravity, `G_A` and `G_B` are gravity acting-object instances. For Coulomb interaction, they are electrostatic acting-object instances.

## 5. The two representations on one target

Let

$$
\mathbf F_{A\to B}
$$

be the ordinary physical pair-force on `B` due to `A`.

There are two VD routes to a contribution on `B`:

1. `G_A` acts on `B`. This is the action side of the `A`-owned representation.
2. `G_B` acts on `A`. The reaction side of this `B`-owned representation lands back on `B`.

So the force account of `B` contains:

```text
IFS(B)

1. action of A-owned G on B
2. reaction of B-owned G on B
```

These are distinct force occurrences because they have different acting-object-instance identities, `G_A` and `G_B`. But they represent the same physical pair interaction on the same target.

If both occurrences were assigned the full ordinary pair-force, then

$$
\mathbf F_{A\to B}
+
\mathbf F_{A\to B}
=
2\mathbf F_{A\to B}.
$$

The same doubling would occur on `A`.

## 6. The half-weighting rule

Each participant-owned representation must therefore return one half of the ordinary physical pair-force.

On `B`:

$$
V\!\left(\operatorname{action}(G_A,B)\right)
=
\frac12\mathbf F_{A\to B},
$$

and, because

$$
\mathbf F_{B\to A}=-\mathbf F_{A\to B},
$$

the reaction of the `B`-owned representation is

$$
V\!\left(\operatorname{reaction}(G_B,B)\right)
=
-\frac12\mathbf F_{B\to A}
=
\frac12\mathbf F_{A\to B}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathbf F^{\mathrm{VD}}_{\text{pair on }B}
=
\frac12\mathbf F_{A\to B}
+
\frac12\mathbf F_{A\to B}
=
\mathbf F_{A\to B}.
$$

Likewise, the two occurrences on `A` sum to

$$
\mathbf F_{B\to A}.
$$

The factor of `1/2` is therefore not a modification of gravity or Coulomb's law. It is the normalization required when one physical pair interaction is represented from both participant-owned sides.

### Near-Earth gravity

If the ordinary force on a body is

$$
\mathbf F_G=-mg\,\widehat{\mathbf y},
$$

then the two VD occurrences on that body are

$$
-\frac12mg\,\widehat{\mathbf y}
$$

and

$$
-\frac12mg\,\widehat{\mathbf y}.
$$

Their sum is

$$
-mg\,\widehat{\mathbf y}.
$$

### Coulomb interaction

For the point-charge Coulomb pair-force, each participant-owned electrostatic representation similarly carries

$$
\frac12\mathbf F^{\mathrm C}_{A\to B}
$$

on the relevant target-side occurrence. Charge signs and geometry still determine the ordinary pair-force. Only the duplicated VD representation is half-weighted.

## 7. Why the theorem and the half-weighting belong in one note

The two-sided representation is not merely chosen for aesthetic symmetry.

The theorem below explains why, for a participant-symmetric acting-object kind, both participants must be capable of owning the same kind. Once both owner-side representations exist, double counting follows unless the values are normalized.

The logical chain is:

```text
owner-blind activation
+
same-kind reciprocity
    ↓
every receiver can own the same acting-object kind
    ↓
the pair is represented from both participant-owned sides
    ↓
each target account contains two representations of one pair interaction
    ↓
each representation carries weight 1/2
```

# Part III — The Owner-Blind Participant Closure Theorem

## 8. Same-kind reciprocity

The symmetry needed here is stronger than the statement that two force values are equal and opposite.

Call `G` **same-kind reciprocal** when:

> If an `x`-owned instance of `G` is admitted on `y`, then `y` can own an instance of that same acting-object kind `G`, and the `y`-owned instance is admitted on `x`.

Schematically:

```text
x.G acts on y
    ↓ same-kind reciprocity
y.G acts on x
```

The acting-object kind must remain `G` after participant exchange.

This excludes a merely paired structure such as:

```text
x owns red-to-blue
and
y owns blue-to-red.
```

Those may produce reciprocal vector values, but they do not preserve one acting-object kind.

## 9. Intuitive proof

Assume:

```text
A.G acts on B.
```

This tells us two things:

1. `A` can own `G`.
2. `B` passes `Condition_G`.

Same-kind reciprocity gives:

```text
B.G acts on A.
```

So we learn two more things:

3. `B` can own `G`.
4. `A` passes `Condition_G`.

Now take two distinct `B`-type participants, `B₁` and `B₂`.

- `B₁` can own `G`, because `B`-type participants were shown capable of owning `G`.
- `B₂` passes `Condition_G`, because it has the same condition-relevant `B` properties as the original `B`.
- The activation condition is not allowed to ask whether its owner is `A`-type or `B`-type.

Therefore

```text
B₁.G acts on B₂.
```

The same reasoning gives

```text
A₁.G acts on A₂.
```

There is nowhere in the activation machinery to insert:

```text
accept B when the owner is A,
but reject B when the owner is B.
```

That would require the condition to inspect the owner and would reveal more than one acting-object kind.

## 10. The theorem

### Owner-Blind Participant Closure Theorem

Let `G` be a nonempty atomic acting-object kind with:

1. an owner qualification `Own_G(x)`;
2. an activation condition `Condition_G(y)` that takes only the non-owner as input;
3. a force-value procedure `V_G(x,y)` that may take both participants as input;
4. same-kind reciprocity under exchange of participants.

Then the class of participants that can own `G` is the same as the class of participants that satisfy `Condition_G`.

If that common class is called `C_G`, then every pair of distinct members of `C_G` admits a `G` interaction.

In particular, if an `A`-type participant and a `B`-type participant interact through `G`, then distinct `A`-type participants can interact through `G`, and distinct `B`-type participants can interact through `G`.

Symbolically, at the type level:

$$
\boxed{
A\leftrightarrow_G B
\Longrightarrow
A\leftrightarrow_G A
\text{ and }
B\leftrightarrow_G B.
}
$$

The type-diagonal does not mean literal self-force. It means interaction between distinct instances of the same participating type.

## 11. Compact formal proof

Here the relation is defined over participant **types or condition-relevant qualification classes**, not over individual identities.

Let

$$
O_G
=
\{X:\text{instances of type }X\text{ can own }G\},
$$

and

$$
T_G
=
\{Y:\text{instances of type }Y\text{ can satisfy }\operatorname{Condition}_G\}.
$$

Because the activation condition does not take the owner type as an argument, the admitted type-level relation is

$$
R_G=O_G\times T_G.
$$

This product form is now derived from the acting-object interface rather than added as a separate assumption.

Same-kind reciprocity makes `R_G` symmetric. For any

$$
X\in O_G,
\qquad
Y\in T_G,
$$

we have

$$
(X,Y)\in R_G.
$$

Symmetry gives

$$
(Y,X)\in R_G,
$$

so

$$
Y\in O_G,
\qquad
X\in T_G.
$$

Hence

$$
O_G=T_G=:C_G,
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{R_G=C_G\times C_G.}
$$

The entire coupled type-class is closed under pairing. A diagonal element such as `(B,B)` means that two distinct `B`-type instances may interact through `G`.

# Part IV — The Red–Blue Test

## 12. A symmetric-looking force that fails the theorem's premise

Imagine the following law:

```text
Red acts on Blue.
Blue acts on Red.
Red does not act on Red.
Blue does not act on Blue.
```

As a bare table of allowed pairs, this is symmetric. The reciprocal arrow exists in each cross-type case.

But it cannot be implemented as one owner-blind acting-object kind while preserving the non-interaction claims.

A single `purple-force` would need to say:

```text
if owner is Red:
    Condition(target) = target is Blue

if owner is Blue:
    Condition(target) = target is Red
```

The condition has queried the owner. Under the proposed interface, that is not one acting-object kind.

The honest decomposition is:

```text
red-to-blue acting-object
    owners: Red
    condition(non-owner): Blue

blue-to-red acting-object
    owners: Blue
    condition(non-owner): Red
```

The exchange

```text
Red red-to-blue → Blue
Blue blue-to-red → Red
```

changes the acting-object kind. It is reciprocal at the vector-pair level but not same-kind reciprocal.

Thus the Red–Blue universe is still logically possible. What the theorem says is:

> It cannot be generated by one atomic owner-blind, same-kind reciprocal acting-object.

This is the precise sense in which gravity and Coulomb interaction are structurally more unified than an arbitrary cross-type-only symmetric force.

## 13. Why a supertype label does not restore the counterexample

One could place both kinds under a common label:

```text
purple-force family
    red-to-blue
    blue-to-red
```

That taxonomic move does not make them one atomic acting-object kind. The evaluator still has to dispatch to different activation conditions according to the owner qualification.

The theorem must therefore be tested at the level where the activation condition and value procedure are actually fixed, not at an arbitrary umbrella name.

# Part V — Structural Admission, Zero Values, and Nondegeneracy

## 14. What the theorem proves immediately

The theorem proves that same-type occurrences are structurally admitted:

```text
B₁ can own G.
B₂ passes Condition_G.
Therefore B₁.G on B₂ is a valid G occurrence.
```

It does not by itself prove that the resulting vector is nonzero in every physical state.

That distinction is important because the force-value procedure may inspect both participants and their geometry.

For example, an admitted occurrence may return zero because:

```text
a coupling parameter is zero in that state;
a geometric factor vanishes;
an orientation factor vanishes;
the participants coincide with a symmetry point;
the vector contributions internal to the value rule cancel.
```

A zero-valued occurrence is not the same thing as no occurrence. The VD must preserve the distinction between **nothing** and a **zero-valued something**.

## 15. The identically-zero branch loophole

A force-value procedure could be written to admit every Red and Blue pair but return zero for all same-type pairs:

```text
Condition_G(target):
    target is Red or Blue

V_G(owner,target):
    nonzero for unlike types
    zero for like types
```

This satisfies owner-blind activation. The theorem correctly concludes that same-type occurrences exist. But their values have been nullified by the value rule.

Therefore there are two possible theorem strengths.

### Structural closure

Under owner-blind activation and same-kind reciprocity:

> Same-type `G` occurrences are admitted.

This is already established.

### Nonzero-potential closure

To conclude that a same-type branch can produce a nonzero force in at least one admissible state, add a nondegeneracy principle:

> **No identically-zero type branch:** if an entire admitted owner-type/non-owner-type branch returns the zero vector in every possible state, that branch is not counted as a genuine interacting branch of the acting-object kind.

This allows ordinary state-dependent zeros while blocking the use of an identically-zero value branch to imitate non-interaction.

The nondegeneracy principle is a candidate addition, not yet established by the present note.

# Part VI — Gravity, Coulomb Interaction, and Biot–Savart Magnetostatics

## 16. Gravity

For the participant-symmetric gravity kind:

```text
Own_gravity(x):
    x possesses the relevant gravitational coupling.

Condition_gravity(y):
    y possesses the target-side gravitational qualification.

V_gravity(x,y):
    use the properties of x and y and their relative geometry.
```

The activation condition does not need to ask whether the owner is Earth, a person, a planet, or another massive participant. The owner changes the numerical input to the value procedure, not the target rule.

Same-kind reciprocity says that a participant which receives gravity can itself own gravity. Hence the owner class and target class coincide.

Once both participants own gravity, the pair receives two participant-owned VD representations and the half-weighting rule applies.

## 17. Coulomb interaction

For the simple point-charge Coulomb kind:

```text
Own_coulomb(x):
    x carries the relevant electric charge/coupling.

Condition_coulomb(y):
    y carries the target-side electric charge/coupling.

V_coulomb(x,y):
    use q_x, q_y, separation, and direction.
```

Again, the condition does not change according to which charged species owns the acting-object. The force value may depend on both charges and may be attractive, repulsive, or zero in a particular state.

A charged participant that receives Coulomb interaction is itself capable of owning the same Coulomb acting-object kind. Hence two charged participants of the same eligible type are structurally admitted to interact.

The participant-owned pair is represented twice in the VD and each representation is half-weighted.

## 18. Biot–Savart magnetostatics

For complete steady-current systems, the same architecture appears across the Biot–Savart source rule and the magnetic-force target rule.

A steady current distribution `A` produces

$$
\mathbf B_A(\mathbf r)
=
\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\int
\mathbf J_A(\mathbf r')
\times
\frac{\mathbf r-\mathbf r'}
{\lVert\mathbf r-\mathbf r'\rVert^3}
\,d^3r'.
$$

A second steady current distribution receives force through

$$
\mathbf F_{A\to B}
=
\int
\mathbf J_B(\mathbf r)
\times
\mathbf B_A(\mathbf r)
\,d^3r.
$$

The same kind of property appears on both sides:

```text
J_A makes A a magnetostatic source.
J_B makes B a magnetostatic target.
J_B also makes B a magnetostatic source.
J_A also makes A a magnetostatic target.
```

A candidate VD interface is therefore:

```text
Own_magnetostatic(x):
    x carries the relevant steady current distribution.

Condition_magnetostatic(y):
    y carries the relevant steady current distribution.

V_magnetostatic(x,y):
    use both current distributions and their geometry.
```

For complete closed steady-current systems, participant exchange preserves the interaction kind, and the owner/target closure theorem applies.

The qualification is important. This should not be read as an equal-and-opposite force theorem for arbitrary isolated current elements, nor extended without argument to time-dependent electromagnetism, where field momentum may be essential.

If the VD represents one complete magnetostatic pair interaction from both current-system-owned sides, the same multiplicity issue arises and the two representations require half-weighting.

# Part VII — Relation to Constitutive Action/Reaction Asymmetry

## 19. A candidate resolution of the gravity stress case

The constitutive-asymmetry sketch distinguishes a route such as:

```text
acting-object state
    -> constitutive law
    -> action on the non-acting participant
    -> negation
    -> reaction on the constitutive carrier
```

A spring or actuator may have a distinguished carrier whose constitutive state generates the pair value.

Gravity and Coulomb interaction do not fit that one-sided picture cleanly. Neither participant is permanently restricted to being the constitutive source or the passive target.

The owner-blind activation rule suggests that this is not an accidental exception. It identifies a separate acting-object class.

### Constitutively asymmetric or relational acting-object

```text
activation may depend on an owner–non-owner relation;
one participant or mechanism may carry distinguished constitutive state;
one side may be computed and the paired side obtained by negation.
```

Examples to investigate include:

```text
spring attachment
normal contact
constraint mechanisms
imposed actuators
```

### Participant-symmetric fundamental acting-object

```text
activation condition queries only the non-owner;
the same acting-object kind may be owned by either participant;
the value procedure uses the joint state;
the pair is represented from both participant-owned sides;
multiplicity normalization is required.
```

Candidate examples are:

```text
gravity
point-charge Coulomb interaction
closed-system magnetostatics
```

Thus gravity need not be treated as a counterexample that destroys constitutive asymmetry. It may reveal that the VD needs at least two acting-object architectures behind the common force-value interface.

## 20. Why the rule is probably not universal

The strict signature

$$
\operatorname{Condition}_G(\mathrm{nonowner})
$$

is not obviously appropriate for every acting-object.

A spring condition such as

```text
the non-owner is attached to this spring
```

refers to a relation involving the owner or constitutive mechanism.

A contact condition usually depends on the relative geometry of both participants.

Therefore this note does not yet propose owner-blind activation as the universal type of all VD acting-objects.

Instead, it proposes owner-blind activation as a defining structural feature of the participant-symmetric fundamental branch. Relational and constitutive acting-objects may require a different activation signature.

# Part VIII — Candidate Selection Principle

## 21. The peculiar empirical pattern

A reciprocal force law could logically have been cross-type only:

```text
A acts on B.
B acts on A.
A does not act on A.
B does not act on B.
```

There is no contradiction in that bare relation.

But under the VD architecture developed here, such a law cannot be one atomic participant-symmetric acting-object kind. It decomposes into different owner-conditioned kinds, or it admits the same-type occurrences and then nullifies them at the value level.

Gravity, Coulomb interaction, and magnetostatic current-current interaction instead have a strikingly unified form:

```text
Can the participant own this kind?
Can the other participant receive this kind?
If yes, evaluate one joint force rule.
```

The same interaction qualification appears on both sides.

## 22. Candidate fundamental-force selection principle

The proposed structural package is:

```text
atomic acting-object kind
+
owner-blind activation
+
same-kind reciprocity
+
optional nondegeneracy
```

From the first three conditions:

```text
owner class = target class
and
same-type occurrences are admitted.
```

With nondegeneracy:

```text
same-type branches must possess at least one nonzero physical state.
```

This does not yet prove that nature must choose gravity or electromagnetic interactions. It does sharpen the selection question:

> Why should fundamental force kinds have owner-blind activation and same-kind reciprocity, rather than decomposing into directed source-species mechanisms?

If those two properties can be independently justified for fundamental acting-objects, the familiar collapse of source and target roles is no longer an additional empirical coincidence. It follows from the VD architecture.

# Part IX — What Changed From d1

## 23. Removed independent assumption

The previous draft assumed

$$
R_G=O_G\times T_G
$$

as a separate source–target factorization principle.

The updated note derives that form from

$$
\operatorname{Condition}_G:
\mathrm{NonOwner}\rightarrow\{\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false}\}.
$$

The algebra is now a compression of the interface, not the source of the insight.

## 24. Removed pair-compatibility loophole

The previous draft allowed one acting-object kind to carry an extra pair predicate such as

```text
accept Red–Blue
reject Red–Red and Blue–Blue.
```

Under owner-blind activation, such a rule cannot change according to the owner. If reciprocal cross-type rules require different target conditions, they must be represented as different acting-object kinds.

## 25. Added structural/nonzero distinction

The updated theorem proves occurrence closure. A separate nondegeneracy principle is needed if the intended conclusion is that every admitted type branch can produce a nonzero force.

## 26. Narrowed scope

Owner-blind activation is presented as a candidate feature of participant-symmetric fundamental acting-objects, not as a universal condition on springs, contacts, and all force mechanisms.

## 27. Unified theorem and half-weighting

The theorem now explains why the symmetric participant-owned duplication exists, and the duplication explains why gravity, Coulomb interaction, and qualifying magnetostatic implementations need half-weighting.

# Part X — Compact Working Summary

## 28. Interface

```text
Condition_G(non-owner)
    decides whether a G occurrence exists.

V_G(owner, non-owner)
    returns its force vector.
```

The condition cannot inspect the owner. The value may inspect both participants.

## 29. Theorem

```text
A.G acts on B
-> B passes Condition_G

same-kind reciprocity
-> B can own G
-> B.G acts on A

another B-type participant also passes Condition_G
-> B₁.G acts on B₂
```

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
A\leftrightarrow_G B
\Longrightarrow
A\leftrightarrow_G A
\text{ and }
B\leftrightarrow_G B
}
$$

at the type level.

## 30. Red–Blue result

A cross-type-only reciprocal force needs two acting-object kinds:

```text
red-to-blue
blue-to-red
```

Calling both `purple` does not make them one atomic kind.

## 31. Half-weighting

```text
participant symmetry
-> A owns G_A and B owns G_B
-> each target account receives one contribution from each representation
-> the same physical pair interaction appears twice
-> each representation carries 1/2
```

Hence

$$
\frac12\mathbf F_{\mathrm{pair}}
+
\frac12\mathbf F_{\mathrm{pair}}
=
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{pair}}.
$$

## 32. Main open questions

1. Is owner-blind activation derivable from a deeper VD rule for fundamental acting-objects, or is it an additional design commitment?
2. Should the no-identically-zero-branch principle be adopted, and how should it be stated without excluding ordinary state-dependent zeros?
3. Exactly which acting-object kinds belong to the participant-symmetric branch?
4. Can the Biot–Savart/current-force case be implemented with the same half-weighted participant-owned structure without losing the closed-system qualification?
5. How should the participant-symmetric branch connect to the action/reaction identity and IFS exclusivity work?
6. Does the weak or strong interaction satisfy any suitable generalization of owner-blind activation and same-kind reciprocity, or do transition rules and multi-body structure require a different architecture?

## 33. One-sentence result

> **For one atomic acting-object kind whose activation condition can inspect only the non-owner, same-kind reciprocity collapses owner and target into one participant class; the resulting two-sided VD representation of each physical pair interaction must be half-weighted to avoid double counting.**
