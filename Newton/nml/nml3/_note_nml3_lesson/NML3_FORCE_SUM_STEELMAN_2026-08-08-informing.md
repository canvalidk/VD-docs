# NML3 Force-Sum Explication — Steel-Man Reading

Status: local supporting note, authored in VD-docs 2026-08-08. Not a transfer
from VD-Newton and not entry wording. It reads
`NML3_WARRANTED_FORCE_ACCOUNT_FOUNDATIONAL_EXPLICATION_2026-08-05.md` at its
strongest in order to separate the moves that are load-bearing from the moves
that are still being uncovered, and to record which apparent gaps the framework
already closes on its own terms.

The explication is work in progress by design. This note is written to support
that work, not to audit it.

---

## 0. The operative principle this reading assumes

The project's working motto:

> If an epistemic idea is computationally irreducible, that is what we call
> ontic.

Epistemic-versus-ontic is therefore not a live axis for NML3, and objections
pitched on that axis do not land. What matters is whether a determination
admits a shortcut. If the only route to a result is to run the procedure, the
result is treated as a fact about the situation rather than a feature of the
modeller's notation.

This principle is what licenses the explication's central claim, and reading
the note without it makes section 5 look like an unearned metaphysical
addition. It is not. It is a restatement of section 2 in the project's own
vocabulary.

---

## 1. What the argument establishes

The chain is short and each link is doing real work:

```text
the forward map from force accounts to resultant vectors is many-to-one
    -> no shortcut recovers the members from the resultant
    -> membership is not constituted by the chosen decomposition
    -> there is a fact of the matter about membership
    -> the law must name the domain on which the sum is performed
```

Section 2 is the proof of irreducibility. Section 5 is the entitlement that
proof buys. They are one argument in two movements, not an empirical claim
followed by a philosophical leap.

This is the strongest structural feature of the explication: it earns the
determinacy of the `interacting-forces-set` **computationally**, from the
non-invertibility of the sum, rather than by assertion. A theory that intends
to be executable should establish its ontology exactly this way.

---

## 2. The load-bearing moves, and what each one earns

### 2.1 The four-way separation

$$
\text{material object}
\neq\text{force-instance}
\neq\text{vector value}
\neq\text{warrant}
$$

Without it the vocabulary cannot state its own problem. Collapsing
force-instance into vector value makes duplicate admission undetectable and
makes two genuinely distinct equal-valued forces into one force. Collapsing
warrant into force-instance makes the admission step invisible, which is
precisely the failure the note is diagnosing in ordinary presentations.

### 2.2 Force-instance identity is not vector-value identity

This is the single most productive distinction in the document. It is what
makes both of the following statable at once, which no value-based account can
manage:

- the same force must not be admitted twice;
- two distinct forces with equal vectors must both remain.

It also forces the IFS to be a set of instances rather than a set of values,
which section 5 correctly notices.

### 2.3 The one-way reading of the constitutive relation

$$
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}(M)
=
\sum_{W_i\in\operatorname{IFS}(M)}
\operatorname{vec}(W_i)
$$

read forward only. The backward reading defines the evidence by the conclusion.
Stating the direction explicitly, rather than leaving it to good manners, is
what turns the equation into something a trace can be graded against.

### 2.4 The "all and only" split into two walls

```text
generator:  Is this candidate a warranted member?
closer:     Are there any further warranted members?
```

Member correctness and set completeness are independent failure modes, they
need different evidence, and an investigator can pass one while failing the
other. Splitting them across two walls is the right factorization, and it is
what stops "list all the forces" from hiding an unexamined instruction.

### 2.5 The behavioural case list

Section 12 is the part of the explication closest to being usable. The
half-vector decomposition, the cancelling pair, the distinct equal-valued
forces, the duplicate instance, the empty account, the nonempty equilibrium
account, and the unknown cardinality are all discriminating tests — a trace can
fail each of them in a visible way. This is the register in which the rest of
the document eventually has to be cashed out.

## 3. Apparent gaps that the framework already answers

Three objections come up naturally on a first reading. Each is answerable from
the text, and recording the answers here should save re-litigating them.

### 3.1 "The identity is false whenever the account is incomplete"

The worry: if a warranted account may be incomplete, then
$\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}=\sum\operatorname{vec}(W_i)$ fails for exactly those
accounts, so the law appears to be stated over an object the investigator never
possesses.

The answer: the relation ranges over **closed** accounts, and closure is a
status the procedure confers, not a state the investigator happens to be in.
An incomplete account is not a counterexample to the law; it is an unfinished
run. This is precisely why closure is a wall and not a footnote — the closer is
the operation that makes the identity assertible in a given case.

No second symbol for "the current partial list" is needed. The distinction is
already carried by closure, which is more economical than carrying it in the
notation.

### 3.2 "The closer is circular, because the only completeness evidence is residual agreement"

The worry: in practice, completeness is established by checking that the
account's sum matches the measured net force — which section 6 forbids.

The answer: section 6 forbids calling an account complete because its members
"have been **adjusted** until their sum agrees." Adjustment is the banned
operation, not comparison. Section 10 stage 5 already draws the line correctly:
agreement between an independently constructed account and an independently
measured net force "can be assessed" but "must not be guaranteed in advance."

That is the difference between fitting and predicting. A residual check applied
to an account generated without reference to the target value is a test the
account can **fail**, and a test that can fail is evidence. The closer is
therefore not unwritable; it is constrained to be falsifiable. That constraint
is a strength, and it is worth stating positively in the wall rather than only
as a prohibition.

### 3.3 "Section 2 proves too much — functional form must count for something"

The worry: if $-kx$ carries no interaction information, controlled variation
seems to be evidentially inert.

The answer: section 2 is a claim about what **algebra alone** delivers, and it
is exactly right at that scope. Section 10 stage 3 then restores functional
form as evidence-in-part: the measured relation "may participate in the
evidence," it simply cannot do all the work by resembling a familiar equation.
The two sections are a deliberate clear-then-rebuild, not a tension.

## 4. Why the triplet is substantive and not merely verbal

A skeptic could say that `interacting-forces-set` and `warranted-force` are so
tightly coupled that co-admitting them is analytic — that the triplet shape is
being satisfied by construction.

Section 13 answers this, and the answer is better than it looks because the two
terms fail in **different** ways when taken alone:

- IFS without the member-kind is word magic: a name for a collection with no
  account of how the correct collection differs from any sum-equivalent one.
- The member-kind without the IFS has no resultant role: warranted instances
  with nothing to be complete relative to, and so no closure problem at all.

Two distinct failure modes means the co-admission is doing work. The triplet is
earned.

The third headword also correctly admits a **member-kind** rather than
requiring members: a particular account may hold zero, one, or many warranted
instances. That is what keeps the empty and equilibrium cases inside the law
rather than as exceptions to it.

## 5. Open leverage points

These are open by intention. They are listed here only because the order in
which they are settled affects how much rework the walls need.

### 5.1 Open item 6 is higher-leverage than its position suggests

> Whether one material interaction can yield more than one relevant
> force-instance in the same account.

Both branches are live and they lead to different generators:

- **One interaction, one instance.** Force-instance identity is carried by the
  relatum. The generator's duplicate-prevention becomes near-free bookkeeping,
  and hurdle 3 from the 2026-08-02 note stops being independently hard —
  it reduces to not admitting the same object twice.
- **One interaction, several instances.** Identity needs its own criterion
  independent of the relatum, and duplicate-prevention stays a first-class
  problem the generator must solve.

Settling this early is cheap; settling it late means rewriting the generator.
The note is right not to guess at it, but it is the next question rather than
the sixth.

### 5.2 The source of the candidate inventory

The closer needs some restriction on which interaction types are eligible
before residual comparison can carry weight, otherwise the space of
undiscovered candidates is unbounded and closure never terminates. The
explication defers this to the later action-reaction and concrete force-law
blocks (open item 10), which is the correct deferral — importing it into NML3
would drag the whole force taxonomy into the force-sum lesson.

Worth recording explicitly, though, that the closer's strength is **inherited**
from that later restriction. The NML3 closer's own job is to state what closure
requires and to remain honest about depending on a candidate inventory it does
not itself supply.

## 6. One terminology observation

Section 6 lists **physical attachment** as a constraint any warrant account
must satisfy: the warrant must connect the force-instance to a relation
involving the target and another relatum.

The predecessor term `attached-force` names exactly that relation. The current
`contributing-force` names the sum role — which is the direction of dependence
the entire document is trying to block. The explication already flags this
vulnerability in its own words: the term "can sound as though appearing in a
successful vector sum is enough to earn force status."

So the framework's own constraint list argues for the term two steps back in
the line `acting-force -> attached-force -> contributing-force`. This is an
observation, not a recommendation — the terminology decision is live at source
in VD-Newton and was deliberately not ingested here.

## 7. What would break if a load-bearing move were dropped

A compact necessity check, useful when the entries get compressed and something
looks droppable:

| Drop this | What breaks |
|---|---|
| Force-instance identity | Duplicate prevention and distinct equal-valued forces both become unstatable |
| Instances rather than values in the IFS | Equal-valued distinct interactions silently merge into one member |
| The forward-only reading | The law becomes self-confirming; any decomposition qualifies |
| The "only" half | Fractional terms, cancelling pairs and invented residuals are admissible |
| The "all" half | Any locally sound partial list counts as the account |
| Generator/closer separation | Member correctness and completeness collapse into one under-specified test |
| Member-kind rather than required members | Empty and equilibrium accounts become exceptions rather than cases |

Every row is a case section 12 already tests for. The case list and the
conceptual apparatus are tightly coupled, which is a good sign for the
explication's internal economy.

---

## 8. Summary

The explication's core achievement is establishing that the force account is
determinate **by showing the resultant does not determine it** — irreducibility
first, entitlement second. Under the project's operative principle that is a
complete argument, and it is a cleaner foundation than the usual appeal to
which forces "really" act.

What remains is mechanism: the identity criterion for one force-instance, the
evidential operation that warrants attachment, and the closure procedure that
inherits its restriction from later blocks. The note itself scopes all three as
open. The conceptual layer looks sound enough to build the walls against.
