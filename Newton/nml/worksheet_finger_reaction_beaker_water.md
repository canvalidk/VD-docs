# Worksheet Question: Pushing a Beaker of Water

A one-litre open beaker has a mass of $200\ \mathrm{g}$. It is filled with one litre of water, which has a mass of $1.00\ \mathrm{kg}$.

A finger is used to give the beaker an acceleration of magnitude

$$
20\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}.
$$

Take the gravitational field strength to be

$$
g=10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}},
$$

and ignore air resistance.

For this idealised question, assume the following:

- When the finger accelerates the beaker **sideways**, all the water moves sideways with the beaker without spilling.
- When the finger accelerates the beaker **downwards**, the beaker moves down faster than the water can fall. The beaker therefore falls away from the water, and the water no longer moves with it.

## Questions

### 1. Sideways motion

The finger accelerates the beaker sideways at $20\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

1. What total mass is being accelerated sideways?
2. Calculate the force that the finger must exert on the beaker.
3. State the magnitude and direction of the reaction-force that the beaker exerts on the finger.

### 2. Downward motion

The finger now accelerates the beaker vertically downwards at $20\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. The beaker falls away from the water.

1. What mass is the finger now accelerating downwards?
2. Calculate the weight of the beaker.
3. Calculate the additional downward force that the finger must exert on the beaker.
4. State the magnitude and direction of the reaction-force that the beaker exerts on the finger.

### 3. Comparison

1. In which trial does the finger experience the larger reaction-force?
2. The finger interacts directly with the beaker in both trials. Why is the reaction-force on the finger nevertheless different?
3. In each trial, identify the two objects belonging to the Newton's-third-law interaction pair involving the finger.
4. A student says, “In the sideways trial, the reaction to the finger pushing the beaker is the water pushing the beaker.” Explain why this is not the correct Newton's-third-law pair.

---

## Answer Key

### 1. Sideways motion

The beaker and water move together, so the accelerated mass is

$$
m=0.200+1.00=1.20\ \mathrm{kg}.
$$

The finger must therefore exert

$$
F=ma=(1.20)(20)=24\ \mathrm{N}
$$

sideways on the beaker.

By Newton's third law, the beaker exerts a force of $24\ \mathrm{N}$ on the finger in the opposite direction.

### 2. Downward motion

The beaker has fallen away from the water, so the finger is accelerating only the $0.200\ \mathrm{kg}$ beaker.

The required net downward force on the beaker is

$$
F_{\mathrm{net}}=ma=(0.200)(20)=4.0\ \mathrm{N}.
$$

The beaker's weight already supplies

$$
W=mg=(0.200)(10)=2.0\ \mathrm{N}
$$

downwards. The finger must therefore supply the remaining downward force:

$$
F_{\mathrm{finger\ on\ beaker}}=4.0-2.0=2.0\ \mathrm{N}.
$$

By Newton's third law, the beaker exerts a force of $2.0\ \mathrm{N}$ upwards on the finger.

### 3. Comparison

The finger experiences the larger reaction-force during the sideways trial:

$$
24\ \mathrm{N}\quad\text{sideways, compared with}\quad 2.0\ \mathrm{N}\quad\text{during the downward trial}.
$$

During the sideways trial, the beaker must transmit force from the finger to the water. The finger must therefore provide the force needed to accelerate both the beaker and the water. During the downward trial, the beaker falls away from the water and no longer has to accelerate it.

In both trials, the Newton's-third-law pair involving the finger is:

- the force exerted by the finger on the beaker; and
- the force exerted by the beaker on the finger.

The force exerted by the water on the beaker belongs to a different interaction pair. Its partner is the force exerted by the beaker on the water, not the force exerted by the beaker on the finger.
