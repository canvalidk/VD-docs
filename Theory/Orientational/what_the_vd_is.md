# What the Valid Dictionary Is Trying to Do

The Valid Dictionary is a formalization of physical theory, but not in the sense that phrase usually carries. Rigorous restatements of physical theories already exist — axiomatizations, category-theoretic reconstructions, Hilbert-style formalizations of mechanics — and the VD is not another entry in that tradition. Its target is different, and the difference is worth naming precisely, because the rules of the framework only make sense once the target is clear.

The VD is a formalization of the *cognitive procedure* a physicist runs when they use a theory to generate a prediction. The thing being faithfully represented is not the mathematical structure of the theory in the abstract, but the act of prediction-making as it is actually performed: the sequence of definitional commitments a practitioner walks through, the recognition inputs they supply along the way, the judgments they make about which entities are present and which rules apply, and the points at which the framework leaves decisions open to be settled by the user rather than by the rules. A physical theory, in this view, is not a static collection of equations but a procedure — a prediction-generating thing — and the VD's job is to render that procedure in a form explicit enough that every commitment it makes becomes visible and auditable.

This target shapes every rule in the framework. Entries must be definitional and append-only because that is how a practitioner's conceptual vocabulary actually accumulates. Laws must be triplets with explicit anchor and winter words because that is how a new concept is actually introduced against prior ones. Recognition inputs must be explicit because the physicist really does, at certain points, stop and decide "yes, this counts" on grounds the formalism cannot supply. Traces must be human-executable because the whole point is to represent what a human does, and any step the evaluator performs that a human wouldn't is a failure of representation, not an optimization. The framework is not trying to automate physics; it is trying to describe what physicists do with enough precision that the description can be checked.

One consequence is that the VD cannot hide anything in background machinery. A conventional formalization is free to rely on the ambient mathematics — set theory, real analysis, whatever — to supply notions of identity, equality, membership, and uniqueness that the formalization itself never has to declare. The VD cannot. If a practitioner, in the course of running a prediction, implicitly commits to the identity of a particle across time, that commitment has to appear somewhere in the entries, because otherwise the VD is representing a cleaner version of the procedure than the one actually being performed. The discipline is unforgiving, and it is unforgiving on purpose: any commitment the framework lets slide into the background is a commitment it has failed to make visible, and visibility is the whole product.

Another consequence is that the VD's outputs have a different epistemic character from those of a conventional formalization. A rigorous restatement of a theory produces theorems about the theory's formal structure. The VD produces something closer to an *audit* of the theory's cognitive procedure — a record of what the practitioner must commit to in order for the procedure to run, written in a form precise enough that those commitments can be counted, compared, and tested against commitments made by other theories' procedures. When the VD surfaces a commitment that had been invisible in standard notation, the claim is not "we have discovered something the mathematics was hiding"; it is "we have discovered something the *practice* was relying on without declaring." These are different discoveries, and they land differently when presented, because the target of the claim is not the formalism but the working method of the field.

This is also why the framework's apparent rigidity — the refusal to let the evaluator do things the physicist would not do, the insistence on recognition inputs where automation would be possible, the preference for traces that can be walked by hand — is load-bearing rather than ornamental. The moment the VD accepts a shortcut the practitioner would not take, it stops representing the practitioner and starts representing some idealized alternative to the practitioner. Whatever results it then produces are results about the idealization, not about the physics-as-practiced, and the audit loses its grip on its target. The constraints are what keep the framework pointed at the thing it is trying to describe.

The short version, then: the VD is a description of physics as a human activity of producing predictions, written at a level of explicitness that makes the activity's implicit commitments countable. It is not a rival to mathematical formalizations of physics; it is an instrument for a different kind of question, one that those formalizations are not built to ask.
