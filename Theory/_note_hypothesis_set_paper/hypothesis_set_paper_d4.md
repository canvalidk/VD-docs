# Where does the theory say to look?

**Can Valid**

**Abstract.** A theory never predicts a bare outcome. When a prediction p is derived from a theory T together with commitments A₁, …, Aₙ, what T entails is the disjunction p ∨ ¬A₁ ∨ … ∨ ¬Aₙ. I call the set of its disjuncts the hypothesis set. The logical kernel is old; what is new is to treat the set as an object fixed by the derivation before the test, whose members are decided one by one. A trial acquits T as soon as one member holds, convicts it only when all have been rejected, and is otherwise undecided. Acquittal is not support. I answer the Quinean objection that there is no such thing as the set, write one set out for an orbit, and read delays before verdicts as the set at work. That leaves a question: what generates the sets of Newtonian mechanics, or of any major theory?

## 1. The patch of sky

On the night of 23–24 September 1846, Johann Galle and Heinrich d'Arrest at the Berlin observatory turned a telescope on a place in the sky that Urbain Le Verrier had written down for them, and found a planet. The place had come from Uranus, which for some twenty years had been falling behind the tables computed for it, and from a calculation. If Newton's law held, and if the Sun and the known planets were the only bodies acting, the tables should have fitted; they did not; so Le Verrier asked where one further Newtonian body would have to be to produce the departure, and in three memoirs to the Académie des sciences in 1845–46 he answered (Le Verrier 1845; 1846a; 1846b). Neptune was found within about a degree of the predicted longitude, by Le Verrier's own reduction.[^1] Thirteen years later he did the same thing again. In 1859 he reported that Mercury's perihelion was advancing some 38 arcseconds a century faster than the Sun and the known planets could account for, and proposed that matter inside Mercury's orbit, a planet or a ring of small bodies, was responsible (Le Verrier 1859). The searches ran for half a century and found nothing; in 1909 W. W. Campbell declared the observational side of the question closed (Campbell 1909). In November 1915 Einstein derived 43 arcseconds a century from general relativity with nothing to adjust (Einstein 1915), and the anomaly has been his ever since.

The two episodes are a textbook pair, and I do not claim them as a discovery. Hanson (1962) built an article on them as the zenith and nadir of Newtonian mechanics; Hempel (1966, pp. 52, 54) set them side by side; Gillies (1993, pp. 98–116) states them in the logical form I will use, one anomaly resolved by changing an auxiliary hypothesis and the other by changing the main theory; Lahav and Massimi (2014) call them the battleground of twentieth-century methodology. What interests me is a question the pair makes visible and the literature has not, so far as I can find, asked in its own right. In both cases astronomers had to go and look before Newton could be judged. Yet nowhere in Newton's theory is the demand stated. Nothing in the law of gravitation or the laws of motion says: if a computed orbit is off, compute where one further body would have to be and examine that region, because if something is there the theory is not at fault, and if nothing is there the theory may be in trouble. The demand is real, it was obeyed twice, and it has no address.

What a theory entails, when a prediction is derived through commitments, is not the prediction but a disjunction, which I call the hypothesis set,[^2] and the demand to examine the patch of sky was one of its members. I argue that such sets exist, that they are definite enough to be written down, and that fields really do work through them, and I end on the problem they leave: for a given theory, what generates its sets?

Section 2 places the idea; Sections 3 to 5 state the set, answer Quine and write one set out; Section 6 reads the delay before verdicts as its signature; Section 7 goes beyond mechanics; Section 8 poses the problem.

## 2. What the literature already says

The logical kernel is old. Duhem (1954 [1906], p. 187) argued that an experiment in physics never condemns one hypothesis on its own but bears on a group of hypotheses together, and does not say which member of the group to change. Quine (1951, pp. 39–40) generalised the point to the whole of science; Section 4 takes up his version. Between the two sits the textbook schema (Hempel 1966, p. 23; Ladyman 2002, pp. 77–81): if H with auxiliary hypotheses A entails I, and I is false, then H or A or both are false. The box in Section 3 is this kernel, written as a disjunction that the theory itself entails before anything has failed.

Popper treated the kernel as a danger to be legislated against: ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses are among the conventionalist stratagems of *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959, §§19–20, pp. 60–62), admissible only if they do not reduce falsifiability; yet in the same book Neptune is a model of theory-guided discovery (p. 90 n. 2). Lakatos (1970, pp. 100–101) answered the worry with a parable in which a Newtonian physicist meets a deviating planet with an unseen planet, then a dust cloud, then a magnetic field, and never recognises a refutation. His methodological falsificationist already fixes by decision, before the test, what counts as unproblematic background knowledge [check], and then decides the conjuncts of a refuted test one after another (pp. 110–111): a list fixed in advance, and the closest prior statement I have found of members decided one by one. He also measured a delay: eighty-five years between Mercury's perihelion being accepted as an anomaly and as a falsification (p. 115). Kuhn (1962, chs VI–VIII) supplies the phenomenology: anomaly need not produce crisis, and Newtonian theory was never seriously questioned over Mercury (p. 81). I call the period during which an anomaly is undecided the window; Kuhn's crisis, when there is one, falls inside it.

The Bayesian treatments of Duhem's problem apportion blame between theory and auxiliaries by priors and likelihoods. Dorling (1979) reconstructed the Moon's secular acceleration with the theory's probability barely moved by the anomaly and the auxiliary's collapsed, as Mayo (1996, pp. 111–112; 1997, p. 227) reports the calculation; Earman (1992, pp. 75, 83–86) disputes the apportioning; and Strevens (2001) shows that auxiliaries about experimental conditions leave the theory's posterior almost unmoved. The standing objection, which Earman (p. 85) concedes, is that the apportioning needs priors on the auxiliaries, and a likelihood for the anomaly under unspecified rivals, that nobody has. Glymour's bootstrap (1980) is derivation-specific and tests hypotheses component by component, but it apportions confirmation among hypotheses. I make no claim about credence, which on an imprecise model moves at once (Joyce 2010). The set concerns when a trial has delivered a verdict, a question neither tradition asks.

Mayo's error statistics comes nearer. Her piecemeal approach (1996, ch. 4; 1997, §4.1) splits the testing of auxiliaries from the appraisal of the primary hypothesis, and blocks blaming an anomaly on an auxiliary unless the auxiliary's falsity has itself passed a severe test, one that would probably have found the claim false had it been false (1997, p. 232; 2018, p. 23). Her hierarchy of primary, experimental and data models (1997, §4) is a list of candidates for blame fixed in advance, but a generic one; the hypothesis set is specific to one derivation and contains search members.

The nearest worry about listing commitments at all is Hempel's. He argued (1988) that applying a theory needs provisos the theory does not vouchsafe, and his example is the force-completeness commitment of celestial mechanics. On Earman and Roberts's reading (1999, pp. 442–445), his point is not that provisos cannot be listed but that they must be stated in the theory's own vocabulary, as "the total force is the gravitational force" is; the stronger worry, which they distinguish from Hempel's, is that the presumptions cannot all be made explicit at once. Section 4 answers that worry from precision.

Eliminative induction comes close from another side: it reaches a theory by refuting its rivals. Earman (1992, ch. 7) argues that Bayesianism must take in eliminative elements at the level of theories (p. 163), and that exploring the space of possible theories has been unjustly neglected (p. 167). Bird (2005; 2010) reads the best cases of inference to the best explanation as Holmesian inference, every rival but one eliminated; Forber (2011) gives elimination the job of framing testing problems; McCoy (2021) argues that meta-empirical evidence can justify eliminative reasoning. Conviction under the rule of Section 3 is eliminative inference too, I concede: Holmesian elimination over the culprits of one derivation, and Bird's medical cases eliminate candidate causes much like commitments. Eliminative inference has a well-known weakness, which Stanford (2006) made into an argument: the list may be incomplete, and historically has been. I take his argument as motivation, not objection (Section 4). The undecided state I describe is a public status of a trial, not an attitude; Friedman's question-directed suspended judgement (2013) is its individual analogue.

Each of these traditions works with a list, but none treats the disjunction that one derivation entails as an object fixed before the test, and none asks what produces it. What I claim is new comes to four items. First, the list is fixed by the validity of one derivation, not by a generic hierarchy (Mayo) or a space of rival theories (Earman, Bird, Stanford), and it includes search members. Second, its members are closed by tests, not by Lakatosian decision, except at stated limits. Third, its three-valued state yields an asymmetry the eliminativist does not draw: acquittal is immediate, conviction needs closure. Fourth, it invites a request nobody has made, for a generator of such lists.

## 3. The hypothesis set

Let a prediction p be derived from a theory T together with commitments A₁, …, Aₙ. I use "commitment" for an auxiliary hypothesis as it appears in an explicit derivation: the frame is inertial, the force account is complete, the arithmetic was right. A theory has a set for each of its derivations; by the theory's sets I mean that map from derivations to sets, and it is the map's generator that this paper asks for. Then:

> **Definition.** Let a derivation of p from T and commitments A₁, …, Aₙ be issued, the Aᵢ including the claims that each step was carried out correctly. Its **hypothesis set** is H = {p, ¬A₁, …, ¬Aₙ}. Each member of H has its own test, and at any time it is *held*, *rejected* or *open*, relative to that test's stated limit and level.
>
> **Verdict rule.** The trial **acquits** T as soon as any member of H is held; it **convicts** T, relative to the stated limits, when every member of H has been rejected; otherwise it is **undecided**.
>
> **Acquittal is not support.** The verdict records only that T was not at fault on this trial. Whether the trial also supports T is a separate question, which the rule does not answer.

For the commitments about the world, T entails the disjunction by the deduction theorem and De Morgan: T ⊢ (A₁ ∧ … ∧ Aₖ) → p, so T ⊢ p ∨ ¬A₁ ∨ … ∨ ¬Aₖ. The members saying that a step was carried out wrongly are of another kind: they are about the derivation, not the world. The whole disjunction is then not entailed by T but true whenever T is true: if the derivation as issued is valid, T entails its object-language part; if it is not, a step-member is true. So a found mistake acquits T: the derivation never linked T to p. Since the trial is individuated by the derivation as issued, it is neither voided nor recoloured when the mistake is found; the corrected derivation is a new trial. So the set is not added to the theory to protect it; it is all the theory says about this trial. "T fails" is not a member, but the name of the state in which every member has been rejected, and the one region the set excludes, ¬p ∧ A₁ ∧ … ∧ Aₙ, is T's empirical content for the situation.

Each member is tested by ordinary means: a suspected mistake by recomputation, a suspected input by re-measurement, a suspected missing body by a search. "Held" means the test came out in the member's favour, the mistake found or the body seen; "rejected" that it came out against. Following Mayo, and symmetrically, a member counts as held only if its test would probably have rejected it had it been false, and as rejected only if its test would probably have held it had it been true, at the stated limit. "Somebody made a mistake" is a family, one member for each step. And the set is fixed before the test: its commitments are those the derivation needs (Section 4), and anyone who could write the derivation out in full could list them in advance. Physicists rarely can or do, since a competent reader supplies the commitments silently, and I think that is one reason the set has gone unnamed.

Acquittal and support must be kept apart. When the member that held is some ¬Aᵢ, the failed commitment accounts for the miss whether or not T is true, and the trial is neutral for T; nor does a run of such acquittals add up to support. When p held, the trial may also support T; whether it does, and how much, depends on how risky p was. That is a different mechanism, which I name and do not model. Severity (Mayo 1996; 2018) and use-novelty (Worrall 2014) are rival accounts of it (Mayo 1991), descended from Popper's corroboration, which he denied was a probability (1959, appendix *ix). Neptune shows the two apart. On the corrected Uranus trial below, the member that held was that an unknown body acts (¬A₃ in Section 5): acquittal only. Le Verrier's inverse derivation of 31 August 1846 was a separate trial, whose point member was a planet near a stated longitude; when it held at Berlin, that trial acquitted Newton and also supported it, as Popper (1963, p. 220) and Worrall (2014, p. 56) both read it. OPERA (Section 6) only acquitted relativity, through a faulty timing chain. Only Neptune was a triumph, because only there did a risky point member hold.

Bouvard's tables of 1821 rested on Newton's law together with the commitment that the forces on Uranus are those of the Sun and the known planets (Alexander 1970), and by the mid-1840s the planet had fallen some two minutes of arc behind them. The first member to hold was a mistake. In November 1845 Le Verrier recomputed the perturbations by Jupiter and Saturn and found terms the old theory had omitted, worth some 29 arcseconds (Le Verrier 1845). That acquitted Newton on Bouvard's trial. The corrected derivation was a new trial, and it missed too. Le Verrier's memoir of June 1846 showed that corrections to Uranus's own elements could absorb only a minor part of the departure,[^3] and set aside a resisting medium, a comet and a planet inside Uranus's orbit, each a version of the force-completeness member; a satellite (¬A₁₀ in Section 5); and an altered law of gravitation, which is a rival to Newton rather than a member of its set (Le Verrier 1846a). What remained was the version whose test was a search, and the memoir of 31 August 1846 gave the single Newtonian body that reproduces the residuals (Le Verrier 1846b). Galle and d'Arrest found it; ¬A₃ held, and the corrected trial also acquitted Newton. On my reading of the figures, requiring one body to produce the residual at every observed moment fixed the direction but not the distance: Neptune lies outside Le Verrier's stated limits, and Benjamin Peirce called the discovery a happy accident (Peirce 1847; note 1). The direction is what the search tested.

Vulcan used the same member, tested by the same kind of search. Newcomb's revision of 1882 made Le Verrier's 38 arcseconds a century nearly 43 (Newcomb 1882). The secular term fixes only a relation between the perturber's mass and distance, the same to first order for a ring as for a body.[^4] So the member pointed nowhere, and each version had to be tried separately (Fontenrose 1973; Roseveare 1982). Newcomb rejected the versions he could test, the solar figure among them (note 5). Hall's altered exponent of 1894 was a rival, not a member (Hall 1894). One version was still open in 1915: Seeliger's zodiacal-light matter of 1906, whose light was seen but whose mass was fitted to Newcomb's residuals rather than measured (Seeliger 1906).[^5] Section 6 returns to what happened in 1915.

## 4. Is there such a thing as the set?

The first reply many philosophers will make is Quine's. Any statement can be held true come what may, if enough is adjusted elsewhere (Quine 1951, pp. 39–40). If so, whatever list of commitments I write down, a defender of T can reach past it into the rest of the web, and there is no *the* set to ask about. Quine later called the claim that the unit of empirical significance is the whole of science a needlessly strong statement of holism (Quine 1991 [check page]; Severo 2012; Verhaegh 2017). I claim that, for a given derivation and a specified T, Duhem's group is definite, and I answer the objection in parts.

*Members are the commitments the derivation needs.* A commitment belongs to the set if and only if T and the other listed commitments do not entail p, to within the stated precision ε, without it. A premise used but not needed is not a member, and adding a commitment makes a different derivation with a different set. Add to the corrected Uranus derivation "one further body, outside Uranus and near the ecliptic, produces the whole residual", and you have Le Verrier's derivation of 31 August 1846, with its own point member and its own new member, that there is no such body. Quine's thesis concerns what may be revised once experience comes in. The set answers a narrower question: what this derivation committed T to beforehand.

*Which derivation is decided by the record, and a gap must be shown.* Different routes to the same p have different sets, and a trial is individuated by the derivation actually issued, a public fact. But published derivations are elliptical, so the full list is a reconstruction, and what disciplines it is a public test. A defender who claims afterwards that the derivation needed a further commitment must exhibit a countermodel, a model in which T and every listed commitment hold and p fails. Larsson and Gill (2004) supplied one for the coincidence window of photonic Bell tests, and so showed that the list had been incomplete. Adding a commitment the inference did not need makes a new derivation; the countermodel requirement keeps rescue from being free. Logic and the meanings of terms are held fixed: the arithmetic carried out is a member, because it can be found wrong, but classical logic is not, which restricts the account's scope without refuting Quine.

*Where T ends is not settled by the derivation.* The set is fixed relative to a specified T, which the derivation does not specify. Whether Hall's exponent is a rival or a member, whether the straight-line propagation of light is part of T or a commitment, changes the set and can change the verdict (Sections 3 and 6 for Hall's exponent, Section 5 for light; Section 7 adds the Lamb shift). In the cases here I take T to be the stated laws, as against parameter values and modelling choices: for the orbit, Newton's laws of motion and gravitation applied to ponderable bodies. That is a residual convention, Lakatos's line between hard core and protective belt under another name. I claim only that once T is stated the rest is not conventional, except at the limits below.

*Finitely many kinds, at a precision.* Against the worry that the presumptions of an application cannot all be made explicit (Section 2), I argue from precision. At precision ε, the commitments whose failure could move p by more than ε fall into finitely many kinds: the forces counted, the inputs, the frame, the reduction chain, the arithmetic. The existential member is finite in kind but infinite in instance, and it closes only to a limit.

*Granularity does not touch the verdict.* "The inputs were right" splits into one claim per input, and so on without end. The regress never reaches the verdict, because statuses compose as a disjunction should: a grouped member is held if any part is held, rejected if every part is rejected, and open otherwise. This strong Kleene disjunction is associative and commutative, and the verdict rule is the same composition applied to the whole set, so regrouping changes the number of members but not the verdict. Where a count matters, members are counted by the tests that close them; whether two tests are independent is a judgement, but only the arithmetic of the window depends on it (note 6).

*The existential member closes only to a limit.* The force-completeness member says that some body acting on the system was left out, without saying which, and no finite test rejects it outright. What a search rejects is a bounded member: no body brighter than a stated magnitude in a stated region. The derivation fixes the form of the test, what an omitted force would do to the residuals; the search fixes how far it reaches, for Mercury to about the eighth magnitude (note 5). A conviction resting on such a rejection says that T fails here, at this precision, given that nothing below the limit supplies the residual. Quine's openness survives as a stated limit, not an unlimited reserve of excuses. A version that can be fitted but not measured stays open, as Seeliger's matter did (Section 3).

*Every closure is a trial, and so is every rescue.* A rejection is the verdict of a further derivation with members of its own; a defender can doubt it, as in Collins's (1985) experimenters' regress, but reopening it is a new trial, on the record. "Held true come what may" describes revision after the fact, which shows up as a changed derivation with its own set, while the original trial stands as decided.

Lakatos's parable is, in these terms, a sequence of derivations, each with an existential member, continuable while some version lies below every limit searched; stated limits make each continuation cost a new, public search, which is where the verdict rule meets his degenerating problemshift. His ceteris paribus clause (pp. 101–102) is the force-completeness member negated, read off the derivation instead of supplied by the defender. Choices of limit and level are conventional, but public, revisable and priced by the next search. I have not removed Lakatos's decision, only located it.

*The set is determinate; the field's list of it need not be.* The field's written list can lag behind the commitments an inference needs. Stanford's (2006) unconceived alternatives are rival theories, and rivals are not members. The set's version of his problem is the unconceived member, a commitment in use that nobody has written down, of which the coincidence window is the clearest case. An unconceived member cannot cause a false acquittal, since an acquittal needs a held member, and a held member is on the list; it can cause a false conviction, so every conviction is conditional on the list. That gap is no reason to doubt that the set exists; it is the reason to ask for a generator.

*One member admits no limit.* Superdeterminism, a violation of the statistical independence of a Bell test's settings from the hidden variables (Section 7), is set aside by convention. Only there can no limit be stated.

There is, then, such a thing as the set: fixed, for a specified T, by the derivation on the record, shown incomplete only by a countermodel, and closed to stated limits.

## 5. One set written out

Here is one set, complete at the level of kinds so far as I can see. The derivation is about as small as celestial mechanics allows: the geocentric right ascension and declination of Jupiter at a time t, from a two-body solution about the Sun with osculating elements at an epoch t₀ a few weeks from t, observed on the meridian, by Gauss's method (Gauss 1809). The precision aimed at is ε, a few seconds of arc, and the arc is stipulated short enough for the other planets to disturb the orbit by less than ε; T is as in Section 4.

The steps: (1) take the six elements at t₀, fitted by least squares to earlier observations; (2) compute the mean motion from Kepler's third law in Gauss's form, n²a³ = k²(1 + m), with k the Gaussian constant and m the mass of the planet and its satellites in solar masses, and from it the mean anomaly at t; (3) solve Kepler's equation, M = E − e sin E, by iteration for the eccentric anomaly E, and from it the true anomaly and radius vector; (4) rotate to heliocentric ecliptic coordinates; (5) subtract the Earth's heliocentric position; (6) correct for light time, taking the planet's place at t − τ, and for aberration; (7) rotate to the equator by the obliquity, and to the true equinox of date by precession and nutation. The theory is used at least twice more: the Earth's place is a Newtonian prediction, and precession and nutation are Newtonian effects of the Sun and Moon on the Earth's bulge. The constant k is not a member: held fixed, it defines the astronomical unit, whose scale re-enters through the solar parallax. The result is the apparent place at a t in uniform time, read on a clock regulated by the Earth's rotation. Meridian readings are reduced for instrumental and clock error, refraction, semidiameter and parallax to an observed place (Bessel 1830; under a relativistic T, Urban and Seidelmann 2013).

| Member | Kind of test | Held | Rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| p: the planet is at the predicted place at t, within ε | Meridian observation, reduced | Within the errors | Outside them |
| ¬A₁ The reference frame is wrong: catalogue or equinox rotates; precession, nutation or obliquity wrong | Dynamical equinox; proper motions; solstices | Rotation or revised constant | Frame and dynamics agree |
| ¬A₂ A known body acts appreciably: another planet, or a star's differential force | Perturbations from masses and distances | Perturbation found | Below ε |
| ¬A₃ An unknown body acts (the search member) | Search where an inverse calculation points | Body seen, reproducing the residuals | Nothing, to a stated limit |
| ¬A₄ A non-gravitational force acts | Mean motions compared across bodies | Such an acceleration found | None, to the precision |
| ¬A₅ The Sun is not a point mass | Solar diameters; figure's effect computed | Effect above ε | Below ε |
| ¬A₆ The elements are wrong: faulty fit, or unfaithful earlier observations | Refit with more observations | Refit moves the place | Agreement within errors |
| ¬A₇ The mass m is wrong | Satellite elongations; perturbations of other bodies | Revised value | Values agree |
| ¬A₈ The Earth's position is wrong (planetary and lunar terms; Earth–Moon barycentre) | The Sun, through the same chain | Solar residuals of matching pattern | Sun where predicted |
| ¬A₉ A propagation or reduction constant is wrong: straight-line propagation; light time; aberration; refraction; solar parallax | Fixed on other objects: Jupiter's satellites, stars, transits of Venus | Constant revised | Confirmed |
| ¬A₁₀ The observed centre is not the centre of mass of planet and satellites: semidiameter, phase, irradiation | Measured diameters; both limbs | Correction found | Below ε |
| ¬A₁₁ The Earth's rotation is not a uniform clock | Sun, Moon and planets compared | Common pattern found | None |
| ¬A₁₂ The instrument or observer is wrong: collimation, level, azimuth, divisions, personal equation; station coordinates | Reversal; fundamental stars; comparing observers | Error found | Clean |
| ¬A₁₃ An arithmetic slip | Independent recomputation | Slip found | Agrees |
| ¬A₁₄ The object observed is not the body computed | Motion over several nights; charts | Misidentification | Identity confirmed |

Every "Held" entry carries one condition: what is found must move the place by more than ε after the elements are refitted with the correction applied, since the elements were fitted to observations reduced through the same chain. For the same reason the refit that tests ¬A₆ is confounded with the tests of ¬A₈ to ¬A₁₂.

Whether this list is the set is a question of validity: step (1) uses the commitment behind ¬A₆; step (2) uses those behind ¬A₇ and ¬A₂ to ¬A₅, which together license two-body motion; every computation uses ¬A₁₃'s; steps (4) and (7) use ¬A₁'s; step (5) uses ¬A₈'s; step (6) and the reduction use ¬A₉'s; the observed side uses those behind ¬A₁₀ to ¬A₁₂ and ¬A₁₄. Drop any one and a model of T and the rest in which p fails by more than ε is easily built: an unseen body, a rotating catalogue, a wrong refraction constant. That sketch shows that each commitment is needed, not that nothing else is.

The trial acquits T as soon as any row is held, and convicts T only when all fifteen are rejected. Rows ¬A₁, ¬A₉, ¬A₁₂ and ¬A₁₃ are families, one member per step. Rows of these kinds have been held: ¬A₃ for Uranus in 1846; ¬A₄ for Encke's comet, whose shortening period Whipple (1950) traced to outgassing, and for the asteroid Golevka (Chesley et al. 2003); and ¬A₁₁, when Spencer Jones (1939) showed that the fluctuations of the Sun, Mercury and Venus, scaled by their mean motions, match the Moon's, the mark of a common clock error (as Brouwer 1952 reports). Every trial whose residual came from that source acquitted Newton.

¬A₁ is kinematic only; a differential force from outside belongs with the forces, in ¬A₂. ¬A₃ is the search member. An unknown body first shows as new observations failing to fit with old, which is also the sign of a faulty fit (¬A₆), and Bouvard, building his tables on the observations of Uranus after 1781 alone (Alexander 1970), in effect held ¬A₆ at ¬A₃'s expense. What belongs to ¬A₃ alone is the search where the inverse calculation points, for a body whose perturbation reproduces the residuals, closed only to a stated magnitude.

Not on the list are T and a rival's content: the derivation does not assume a relativistic perihelion advance absent but assumes Newton's law, and "there is a relativistic advance" is the form "T is false" takes when someone has a rival. Light shows where T ends deciding the matter (Section 4). With T restricted to ponderable bodies, straight-line propagation is a commitment, in ¬A₉; if T covers corpuscles of light, a deflection is T's own content, about 0.84 arcseconds at the Sun's limb on Soldner's calculation of 1801 (Sauer 2021), one of the values the eclipse of 1919 tested (Dyson, Eddington and Davidson 1920). Either way the clause is idle here: at any elongation at which a planet can be observed on the meridian the deflection is far below ε.

A generator of sets would have to meet four conditions. First, it must yield the force-completeness member, that the only appreciable force on the planet is the Sun's attraction as a point mass, whose parts are ¬A₂ to ¬A₅, and say what the search in ¬A₃ is for. The table has it only because I walked a derivation I already understood, which shows that the target is definite but is no generator, being neither mechanical nor extensible. Second, regrouping invariance: ¬A₂ to ¬A₅ could be one member with four tests, with the same verdicts. Third, it must say where the specified T ends, as light shows. Fourth, it must yield at least the Bell loopholes now listed (Section 7), and ideally those not yet named.

## 6. The delay as evidence

If sets exist and fields work through them, there should be a signature: after an anomaly, a period without a verdict while members are open, filled with member tests. That is what the record shows. A delay alone is weak evidence, since Kuhn, Lakatos and any Bayesian with a high prior on T also predict a period of checking auxiliaries. What the account adds is a date: the verdict comes when the set closes, flat and then a step, not by gradual drift. That can be checked against public verdicts, in declarations, retractions, textbooks and prizes.

A window that ends in conviction lasts as long as the slowest member, since a trial convicts T only when every member has been rejected; more independent member tests then make a longer window, and this is arithmetic, not attachment.[^6] A window that ends in acquittal lasts only until the first member is held.

A found mistake is an acquittal plus a rerun. The mistake member holds, and T was not at fault (Section 3); but everyone corrects the derivation and redoes the experiment, which is a new trial with its own set, and if it misses, its own window runs. So a real anomaly first blamed on a mistake reaches the theory one rerun and one window later, as Neptune did (Section 3).

OPERA is the clearest recent case: on 22 September 2011 the collaboration reported that muon neutrinos sent from CERN reached Gran Sasso about 60 nanoseconds earlier than light would have, a six-standard-deviation effect (Adam et al. 2011).[^7] The point member had been rejected beyond the field's threshold for discovery.[^8] The collaboration claimed no failure of relativity and offered no interpretation (CERN 2011). On 23 February 2012 CERN named two candidate faults, among them a fibre connector to the master clock not fully seated. The connector member held when its shift, about 73 nanoseconds, was confirmed by timing horizontal muons through the LVD and OPERA detectors (Agafonova et al. 2012), and the trial acquitted relativity. The corrected analysis (Adam et al. 2012) was a new trial, and so were the reruns by ICARUS (Antonello et al. 2012) and OPERA (Adam et al. 2013), all consistent with light.

The solar-neutrino problem is a longer window that tests the date. Davis, Harmer and Hoffman (1968) reported an upper limit of 3 solar neutrino units from the Homestake detector against a prediction of 7.5 ± 3 (Bahcall, Bahcall and Shaviv 1968), and the rate later settled at about a third of the solar model's. Take as T the standard account of the neutrino, massless and unmixed. Of Bahcall's (2004) three classes of explanation, the solar model and the experiment are members; the neutrino physics is what is left when both are rejected. The experimental member was rejected after recovery tests on the extraction chemistry (Davis 2003) and when Kamiokande-II saw a deficit, about half the predicted ⁸B flux, in real time and pointing back to the Sun (Hirata et al. 1989; 1990). The standard solar model was rejected as the culprit chiefly by the pattern of deficits across energies (Haxton 1995) and by helioseismology (Bahcall et al. 1997). If the solar member closed there, the rule convicts T by 1997, and the field's declaration, which came from SNO (Ahmad et al. 2001; 2002), lagged it by four years. I think the better reading is that the solar member was existential, some non-standard model yielding fewer ⁸B neutrinos, closed only to a limit until SNO's neutral-current measurement found the total flux of all flavours at the solar model's value; the declaration then came at closure. That reading is made after the fact, and verdicts that drifted down before closure would count against the account.[^9]

Newcomb's tables look at first like a counterexample. They added to each inner planet's perihelion the motion an exponent of Hall's form would produce, n = 2.0000001612 (Newcomb 1895, p. 184), and from 1900 they were generally adopted for the national ephemerides (Duncombe and Seidelmann 1978, pp. 3–4). Yet the force-completeness member was still open: the planet search ran until 1909, and Newcomb left a ring of small bodies between Mercury and Venus to future photometry, while judging it unsatisfactory (§60, p. 118). He declared no verdict, calling Hall's hypothesis provisionally "not inadmissible" (§61, p. 118), applying the correction "as if" the planets gravitated so (p. 184), and naming the Moon as the test (§61, p. 119), which Brown carried out against the exponent in 1903 (Brown 1903). A rival was adopted for computation inside an undecided trial. The account forbids a verdict while a member is open, not accommodation.

The structure also says where the danger lies. Beyond the false convictions that unconceived members can cause, exposed only when they are conceived and tested (Section 4), the graver error is a false acquittal: a mistake wrongly believed found, or a member wrongly judged held by a test that could not have rejected it. Only the first is exposed by the rerun, and the larger the set, the more members can hold falsely. Section 7 discusses a contested candidate. What a tally of verdicts measures is set out in a note.[^10]

The account's main limitation is Vulcan in 1915. Seeliger's member was open, yet the community treated Newton as convicted once a rival produced the figure with nothing to adjust. The rule as stated does not license that, since a rival's success is not a test of any member of Newton's set. The likely repair is that a rival's set can close Newton's open member: general relativity's derivation of the 43 arcseconds carries its own force-completeness commitment, that no appreciable intra-Mercurial matter acts, and on this reading the success of that derivation closed the one member Newton still had open. It would do so through the separate mechanism of Section 3, a risky point member held with nothing to adjust, since an acquittal of general relativity alone would decide nothing about Newton's set. I state this as the likely repair, not as a result. Working it out means saying when a trial of one theory may decide a member of another theory's set, which the rule as stated does not do; until then Vulcan is the place where rule and history part.

## 7. The question beyond mechanics

Nothing in Sections 3 to 6 is special to Newton. Take the set-up Maxwell described to Tait on 11 December 1867 (Maxwell 1867): gas A is hotter than gas B, at temperatures Θ_A > Θ_B, and the partition between them has a small hole. I follow the statistical second law as Smoluchowski and Szilard posed it, the demon a physical system. T: no cyclic process systematically lowers the entropy of an isolated system, fluctuations aside. Heat Q passing from B to A would change the entropy by Q/Θ_A − Q/Θ_B < 0, so p: no sustained net flow of heat from cold to hot without compensating change elsewhere. The commitments: the composite is isolated; molecules cross one for one, so only energy is transferred; each gas stays in internal equilibrium; demon and door return to their initial state each cycle; the entropy account is complete, every part whose state changes entered in the sum; the arithmetic is right. One member is existential, as force completeness was: something carrying entropy was left out.

Maxwell's being sorts the molecules so that A grows hotter without work. The trial was undecided because p itself was open, never rejected by observation, and it was carried on in argument, which stretches an apparatus built for empirical trials. Two kinds of answer were given. Smoluchowski (1912), and later Norton (2013), argued that the demon does not sort, since a trapdoor at the gas's temperature fluctuates as the molecules do: the point member holds. Szilard (1929; an entropy cost of k ln 2 in measurement), Brillouin (1951; the light needed to see a molecule) and Bennett (1982) each proposed a version of the existential member. Bennett, applying Landauer's (1961) bound of kΘ ln 2 per erased bit at temperature Θ, argued that measurement can be reversible but the demon's memory must be reset before the cycle closes, and erasure pays: his member is the failure of cyclicity made specific. That is the usual textbook resolution, on which the member held and the second law was acquitted. But Landauer derived the bound from the second law, and Bennett grants that the principle is in a sense a restatement of it (Bennett 2003). On Earman and Norton's dilemma (1999), the exorcism either assumes the second law or rests on a principle about information that lacks independent justification. Experiments since 2010, which could have failed, confirm that information can be turned into work (Toyabe et al. 2010; Koski et al. 2014) and that erasure costs at least kΘ ln 2 (Bérut et al. 2012; Jun, Gavrilov and Bechhoefer 2014); whether that defeats every demon is what Earman and Norton dispute, and the experiments' analysis uses stochastic thermodynamics, whose standing is part of the question. So the acquittal is a candidate false one, contested rather than shown. Is the demon's list the same for every second-law derivation, and does the theory's structure license it, or its defenders' ingenuity?

In quantum physics one community has written a set down, though not in advance, and not for quantum mechanics. A Bell test derives, from local realism and commitments about the apparatus and analysis, an inequality that quantum mechanics violates. The commitments came to be called loopholes: detection, locality, memory, coincidence-time and freedom of choice, the five that Kofler, Giustina, Larsson and Mitchell (2016) list. With local realism as T, the forty-three years from Freedman and Clauser (1972) to 2015 were a run of undecided trials, each with a member left open, until three experiments in 2015 rejected detection and locality in one trial (Hensen et al. 2015; Giustina et al. 2015; Shalm et al. 2015).[^11] Freedom of choice closes only to a bound, the cosmic tests pushing any common cause into the deep past (Handsteiner et al. 2017; Rauch et al. 2018); superdeterminism admits no bound. Larsson (2014) singles it out as the one loophole that scientific methods cannot close, and the field sets it aside by convention (Section 4), as Hossenfelder and Palmer (2020), who dissent, document. The 2015 verdict is a conviction relative to that convention.

The Bell list is the set of one derivation, and it belongs to quantum mechanics' rival. Quantum mechanics' own sets for ordinary predictions are nowhere written down, but one can be. Take as T Dirac's equation for an electron, and as p its prediction that the 2S₁/₂ and 2P₁/₂ levels of hydrogen coincide, within ε. Tested as Lamb and Retherford (1947) tested it, by microwave quenching of metastable 2S atoms in a magnetic field, the commitments are: (1) the only interaction acting on the electron is the Coulomb field of a point proton, the force-completeness member again, and existential; (2) the hyperfine structure is allowed for; (3) no stray electric field shifts or quenches the levels; (4) the atoms detected are metastable 2S hydrogen; (5) the microwave frequency and the magnetic field are measured right; (6) the Zeeman theory that carries resonance fields back to zero field is right, which uses T again, as the Earth's place did for Newton; (7) the arithmetic is right. Lamb and Retherford's account raises most of (2) to (5), and their introduction names versions of (1) and a rival, listing what more accurate data would bear on: the relativistic wave equation, which is T, coupling to the radiation field, and a non-Coulombic interaction between electron and proton.[^12] They found 2S₁/₂ higher by about 1,000 megacycles per second. Bethe (1947), noting that others had shown a nuclear interaction and vacuum polarisation inadequate, obtained about 1,040 megacycles from the electron's interaction with the quantised radiation field, absorbing the most divergent part into the observed mass.

Which verdict this is depends on what T was: missing interaction or rival theory? If T is Dirac's equation for one electron in a Coulomb field, the radiative term is a rival's content, as the relativistic advance is for Newton, and with the other versions of member (1), a nuclear interaction and vacuum polarisation, already rejected as too small, the trial convicts T at this precision. If T is Dirac's radiation theory, which already contained the coupling, the radiative term is a version of member (1) that the derivation left out, and the trial acquits T. But that theory gave the term as infinite, and a finite value needed what Bethe calls a proper identification of terms, so the second T was itself being amended. The derivation does not settle where the theory ends (Section 4); a generator would have to. On that reading member (1) counted as held only when relativistic calculations gave 1,051 to 1,052 megacycles (Kroll and Lamb 1949; French and Weisskopf 1949) and the same coupling accounted for the electron's magnetic moment (Kusch and Foley 1948; Schwinger 1948).[^13] Quantum mechanics' sets for ordinary predictions are as definite as Newton's and, outside reconstructions like this one, as unwritten.

## 8. The problem

A theory's hypothesis sets are definite objects. Each is fixed by the derivation used and the T specified, shown incomplete only by a countermodel, closed to stated limits and, where a member is undecidable, by convention, and each is in place before any observation is made. Experimental papers already write partial sets: a systematic-uncertainty budget, such as OPERA's of 2011, lists commitments of the apparatus and the analysis with their sizes (Adam et al. 2011). But at the level of the theory, as opposed to the experiment, no field writes down its theory's sets for ordinary predictions; the sets written out here are reconstructions after the fact.

If all of this is obvious once said, and I think most of it is, the question it leaves is plain. Since a set belongs to a derivation, the question is for a generator: something that takes a specified theory and a derivation as the field actually writes it, with commitments supplied silently, and returns the full set, tacit members included, with the form of each member's test, before the observation is made. Two parts are hard: the unconceived members, and the tests of the existential members, above all the form of a search. I can say what an answer must involve, and no more. The member that acquitted Newton in 1846 comes from the universality of the law, every body attracting every body, which makes an unaccounted residual the signature of an unaccounted body; from the rule, prior to any equation, that every force has a source; and from the record of residuals, which is not part of the theory and yet supplied the member's content. The four conditions of Section 5 would show that one had been found. That is a well-posed problem, and I pose it rather than attempt it.[^14] What generates the sets of Newtonian mechanics, and where, in each derivation, does the theory say to look?
