## 0. Short version

> Everything we have until the Force-Sum law is pretty good. And we'll use the
> haze and contributions version. *(stated)*

1. **The regress.** A prediction made from a gravity term carries that term's
   uncertainty and nothing else. When a drop experiment comes out wider and
   slightly off, adding an air-resistance term does not remove the problem. It
   moves it to the next level of instrument precision. At every finite stage
   the account is a keyed list plus a claim about the rest. Haze is that
   claim, written as a term. *(his argument; §§1–2)*
2. **Which side expands.** Contributions unfold; haze stays fixed within a
   trace. Three arguments: haze contains no keyed force to unfold; only the
   contribution recursion keeps every line true at the one tolerance the asker
   gave; and only it is independent of the order of traversal. *(his lean;
   the arguments are analysis; §3)*
3. **Two axes.** The Taylor-style recursion on the remainder is real, but it
   is the step from one tolerance to a tighter one. That step is a new
   question and a new trace. *(analysis; §4)*
4. **What the baseball needs.** For enough data to count against Newton, haze
   must be barred from holding reproducible structure, not only bounded in
   size. *(analysis, not ruled; §6)*

## 1. The drop: wanting a win for Newton

> Haze allows you to say that, even though my gravity experiment didn't match
> my predictions, that's because of some other contributions I didn't consider
> yet. (I'm imagining Galilean drop experiment ) "I had already considered the
> uncertainty due to my gravity measurements, so I was expecting uncertainty.
> But the reality was more distributed and slightly off of my uncertainty
> measurement of gravity would imply. And yet, I want to consider this
> experiment as a win for Newton. How can I do that? The uncertainty of gravity
> follows from the gravity equation I used. I must be able to say, deviation is
> small. *(stated)*

Three things are in this passage *(analysis)*.

**The gravity uncertainty has the gravity term's shape.** It is uncertainty
about one keyed term, so it can only move the prediction the way that term's
parameters move it. If one value of $g$ is used for every drop, an error in it
shifts every fall time the same way, $\delta t/t=-\delta g/2g$. It predicts no
scatter from drop to drop. So "more distributed" is not more of the predicted
uncertainty. It is a departure of a kind the gravity term cannot produce, and
no error bar on $g$ absorbs it. This is the 10 September distinction between
the accuracy of the included contribution and the sufficiency of the account,
seen in one experiment.

**"Deviation is small" does not follow from the gravity equation.** He says so
himself: the uncertainty of gravity follows from the gravity equation, and the
smallness claim is a further thing he "must be able to say". It is a separate
commitment with its own content.

**It has to be said beforehand, with a size.** Said after the result, it is
the Major Logical Failure of the 10 September record: a different proposition
substituted for the one tested. Said before, it is part of the prediction, and
the prediction held. That is what makes the experiment a win. The size also
sets what the win is worth. A wide allowance is easy to meet and shows
little; a tight one that is met shows more.

## 2. The regress

> And now, someone could think some extra uncertainty term isn't necessary,
> they might say, just add the term form air ressistence. But then they would
> only have pushed the problem down to appearing when measuring equipment
> improves. *(stated)*

*(analysis)* "Just add air resistance" treats the gap as one forgotten term.
Add drag, and a better instrument exposes buoyancy; add buoyancy, and the next
one exposes the Earth's rotation, then the tide. The objection can be made
again at every stage, and it never ends the series. At every finite stage the
account has the same form:

```text
net force = (the keyed terms listed so far) + (everything not listed)
```

Adding a term changes what falls under "everything not listed". It never
removes that part. So the claim about the unlisted part is not a patch for an
incomplete list. It is a fixed feature of any finite list, and a prediction
made from a finite list either includes a statement about it or says nothing
about the net force.

This is the same fact as Logical Consequence 10, from the other side.
Generation gives existential witnesses and closure needs a universal negative
("no eligible acting-object remains"), which no finite search discharges. The
regress shows the universal negative is also false in every real experiment.
The 13 September record's replacement stands: the closer is asked for a
bounded claim about the aggregate of everything not admitted, which can be
supported and can be refuted.

The Taylor case is the familiar instance. Nobody thinks writing the next term
of a series makes the remainder term unnecessary. The remainder is part of
what makes the truncated statement true.

**Why exact closure does not escape** *(analysis, using the verdict rule of the
hypothesis-set paper, d4 §3)*. Suppose the account claims exact completeness.
That commitment is false in every real scene, so its negation, "something was
left out", is a member that always holds. Every miss then acquits Newton at no
cost. An account with exact closure is as unfalsifiable as one with a free
residual, by the opposite route. The paper already answers this "from
precision": membership is decided "to within the stated precision ε". Haze is
the object in the trace that carries that precision.

## 3. Which side expands

> Do we expand haze into terms or contribution into terms. In other words, is
> it haze_n = acting_object_force + haze_n_1, or contribution_n =
> acting_object_force + contribution_n+1. I think there is a right answer. Ok
> so taking it from Taylor expansion, pay attention to this: for a taylor
> expansion statement to be true we have: F(x) = terms + error, where error <
> some fixed amount. Now what we want to produce is a series of sentences that
> are each true as we expand it. If we pick a fixed error first, that would
> determine how many terms we need as a mathematical consequence... im leaning
> towards contribution being expanded, but I feel a little uncomfortable
> without explaining it to myself well enough yet. *(stated)*

The two candidates:

```text
(H)  haze_n         = impressed-force_(n+1) + haze_(n+1)
(C)  contribution_n = impressed-force_(n+1) + contribution_(n+1)
```

*(analysis)* (C) is right, for three reasons.

**1. Type.** Haze is by definition the unkeyed part of the account (13
September: the extra piece "is unkeyed. It has no identity, only a declared
set it must lie in"). Recursion (H) takes a keyed force out of something that
contains no keyed force. A keyed force can only be unfolded from something
already made of keyed forces. That is the 10 September definition of a
contribution: "a non-random identifiable superposition of impressed forces",
whose identity and composition are fixed before its value is.

**2. Every line true, at one tolerance.** With the error fixed first, as he
says, recursion (C) gives

$$
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}
=Z_\epsilon+\mathbf F_1+\dots+\mathbf F_n+\mathbf C_{\mathrm{remaining}}
$$

at every step. Each line is true, and each is true at the same $\epsilon$, the
one the asker supplied. The recursion ends when the remaining contribution is
an exact empty sum. That zero is the exact zero of the 10 September record's
three zeros, and it is a different object from $Z_\epsilon$. The 9 September
puzzle of expansions ending in "+ 0 + 0 + 0" came from not having the two
apart.

Under (H) the first line is $\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}=\mathrm{haze}_0$, where
$\mathrm{haze}_0$ contains gravity. Read with haze's meaning, that line is
false. It can be made true only by giving $\mathrm{haze}_0$ a bound wide enough
to hold gravity, which is not a tolerance. The bound then changes on every
line, so each line answers a different question and only the last answers the
asker's.

**3. Order.** Taylor's remainder shrinks at each step because the terms come
ordered by a small parameter. An interaction-set is unordered (Logical
Consequence 11: no intrinsic final force), and its members can be large and
cancel (Logical Consequences 6 and 14). Under (H), "stop when the haze is small
enough" depends on the order of traversal, and it can stop before two large
keyed forces that cancel, leaving them in haze. Under (C) every order of
traversal gives the same final sentence.

**The September records already took (C).** The 9 September record has
successive remainders replace one another, with the final one "an exact
empty-list result" and the unresolved aggregate "represented once". The 10
September terminology note renames the remainder "contributions" and writes
$\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}=Z_\epsilon+\mathbf F_{\mathrm{gravity}}+\mathbf C_{\mathrm{remaining}}$.
What is new here is the argument for the direction, which those records do not
give.

## 4. Two axes

*(analysis)* The Taylor recursion on the remainder,
$R_n=\text{term}_{n+1}+R_{n+1}$, is not wrong. It describes a different move:
going from a tolerance $\epsilon$ to a tighter $\epsilon'$. A member is then
promoted out of haze into the contributions. This is the 13 September table's
row for "to first order": "successive remainders nest — the haze at one order
contains the haze at the next."

| | Within one trace | Between traces |
|---|---|---|
| What is fixed | $\epsilon$, the question, the keyed set | the scene |
| What unfolds | contributions, by entry expansion | haze sheds a member |
| What kind of step | definitional | a new tolerance or a detection: a new question |
| Where it ends | exact empty sum | it does not (§2) |

So both recursions exist. Contribution unfolds inside a trace. Haze sheds
terms between traces. The regress of §2 is the second axis followed without
end, and it is the reason the first axis must carry a haze term at every
stage.

This also answers the worry in §2 about better equipment. A tighter instrument
sets a new tolerance, which makes a new prediction and a new trial. The earlier
win stays a win, because it was a win at its own tolerance.

## 5. Where the Taylor analogy breaks

*(analysis)* In Taylor's theorem a fixed error determines the number of terms.
He draws on exactly this ("that would determine how many terms we need as a
mathematical consequence"). For forces it does not hold in that form. A
tolerance constrains what may be left out: the aggregate of the unlisted part
must lie in the declared set. It does not pick out one keyed set. Several
keyed sets can satisfy the same tolerance, and a pair of large cancelling
interactions can be in or out (12 September: the aggregate is what the account
depends on).

So "which interactions are explicit at $\epsilon$" is still a modelling input.
Under the wall-landing discipline it has to land at a wall. What haze changes
is that the input becomes checkable: the chosen set is adequate if what it
leaves out stays within the allowance.

## 6. What the baseball needs haze to be

> A case to reveal this might be to consider an experiment where a thrown
> baseball under normal conditions is being measured to disprove Newton via
> relativity. Technically, with enough data, such a thing would be possible,
> but not if for every discrepancy, the scientist introduces a new
> contribution. *(stated)*

*(analysis throughout; the numbers are computed here and not checked against a
source.)* Treat gravity as an impressed force under special-relativistic
dynamics, which is crude but shows the structure. Then
$m\mathbf a=\big(\mathbf F-(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v)\mathbf v/c^2\big)/\gamma$,
and to leading order

$$
m\mathbf a-\mathbf F\approx-\frac{v^2}{2c^2}\Big[\mathbf F+2(\mathbf F\cdot\hat{\mathbf v})\hat{\mathbf v}\Big].
$$

At 40 m/s, $v^2/2c^2\approx9\times10^{-15}$. The residual is about
$10^{-14}$ N against a weight of 1.4 N, with drag near 1 N.

**Two things must both hold for the data to count.**

- *Minting must cost something.* This is the contribution/decomposition
  distinction of the August lessons. A decomposition term commits to nothing
  beyond the sum. A contribution is keyed to an acting-object instance, so it
  commits to a source, conditions of action, a law and a counterpart. The
  lessons say membership must be warranted "independently of their ability to
  reproduce the measured net force". Put shortly: identity is never taken from
  the sum, though a value may be (the remainder route does that for a member
  already identified).
- *Haze must not be allowed to hold the residual for ever.* If haze is only a
  ball of radius $\epsilon$, normal conditions cannot support an $\epsilon$
  near $10^{-14}$ N, so the residual sits inside haze at any volume of data,
  and the scientist does not even need to mint anything. "With enough data"
  works only if anything that replicates as structure must be keyed. Then
  averaging beats magnitude and the pattern has to be accounted for.

**So haze does two jobs**, which the September records do not separate:

1. the bound makes "adequate for this question" decidable;
2. a no-reproducible-structure clause makes "everything reproducible must be
   keyed" enforceable, and this is what lets data count against the theory.

His 10 September wording, a contribution as a "non-random" identifiable
superposition, already implies the second. The 12 September car record cuts
against it ("Haze can contain a persistent, directed frictional contribution.
It need not be random at all"). The 13 September table reconciles them: a
named-and-neglected force is "*not* haze" but "a keyed member at nominal zero,
with a bound from its own law". On that reading there are three classes:

```text
keyed and valued        a contribution with its own uncertainty
keyed and neglected     a member at nominal zero, bounded by its own law;
                        may be persistent and directed
unkeyed (haze)          bounded, and holding no reproducible structure
```

In the drop experiment of §1 the slight offset is the second kind of
departure. If it replicates, haze may hold it at today's tolerance, but it is
owed a key.

**Why loosely known drag cannot hide the residual either.** The uncertainty of
a keyed term lies only along its own law's parameters. The uncertainty of a
free decomposition term is the whole vector space.

**Why the residual cannot be keyed once exposed.** It has no source to remove,
shield or vary. Its law takes the target's velocity in the frame and the sum
of the other members as arguments, not the relative state of two
participants. Nothing receives a reciprocal. In a closed system it is a leak
with nothing outside to pair it with. Each failure belongs to a different
later block (the Force-Sum walls, acting-object, action–reaction, mechanical
closure), none of which has entries yet. The residual's signature,
target-only quantities times the net force, points back to the Newton II
house.

## 7. The same point as a wall count

*(analysis; rests on §6 of the 1 November triplet record, which was offered and
not ruled.)* Net force comes from the chimney. The account must supply the
rest, and it has two outward groundings available: the keying of contributions
and an independent restriction on haze.

| Grounding | What results |
|---|---|
| Neither | free decomposition, $\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}=\mathbf G+(\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}-\mathbf G)$ |
| Keying only | haze is defined as the leftover (the 12 September boxed $\mathbf H_{\mathbf C}=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}-\mathbf C$ with no restriction); any miss is haze |
| Restriction only | contributions are whatever keeps haze in bound: a new contribution for every discrepancy |
| Both | one condition that can fail: $\mathbf F_{\mathrm{net}}-\mathbf C$ must lie in the declared set |

The two ways the account can be made unfalsifiable are the two one-grounding
rows.

   no-reproducible-structure clause of §6. The baseball case needs the third.
   Not ruled.
2. **What "reproducible structure" means exactly**, and the test that detects
   it. The 11 September brief's two detection claims (a nonzero omission; an
   omission beyond the allowance) look like the two jobs of §6. Not worked
   through.
3. **The explicit set at a tolerance** (§5): a wall input, or a rule.
4. **Value from the sum.** A constraint member such as a string has a value
   that is "whatever balances". What separates it from the illegal move seems
   to be that it brings a kinematic constraint of equal dimension, a
   direction, a sign condition and a counterpart. No record states the rule.
5. **Relational arguments.** That an acting-object's law takes the relative
   state of its participants, and never the aggregate or the frame velocity,
   is used in §6 and stated in no entry.
6. **Inequalities as peripheral entries.** Haze's restriction is one. Open
   item 1 of the 1 November triplet record bears on it.
7. **Whether haze is a triplet headword** (net-force / contributions / haze)
   **or the content of the closer wall** under the ratified names. His
   instruction is to "use the haze and contributions version"; the cut is not
   decided here.
8. **Whether the role must be a term.** §2 shows the role is permanent. The
   reason to make it a term is that the trace has to carry it from force to
